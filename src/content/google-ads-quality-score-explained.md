# Google Ads Quality Score Explained (And How to Actually Improve It)

Quality Score is the metric most advertisers have heard of, fewer have looked at, and almost nobody has acted on. It sits hidden behind a column setting in the Google Ads interface, which is a strange place for something that directly affects what you pay for every click.

I'm Hitesh Jaganiya, a digital marketing consultant certified in Google Ads and Google Analytics. This is a plain explanation of what Quality Score actually is, what the three components mean, and what genuinely moves it — as opposed to the vague advice that usually surrounds this topic.

## What Quality Score Actually Is

**Quality Score** is a 1-10 diagnostic rating Google assigns to each of your keywords, estimating how relevant and useful your ads and landing pages are to someone searching that keyword.

Two things about that definition matter more than people realise.

First, it's **per keyword**, not per campaign or per account. You can have a keyword scoring 9 and another scoring 3 in the same ad group.

Second — and this trips up a lot of people — the number you see in the interface is a **diagnostic tool**, not the actual score used in the auction. Google calculates ad quality in real time for every single auction, factoring in things the reported number doesn't capture: the exact search query, the user's device, their location, the time of day. The 1-10 figure is a historical summary to help you find problems. It's genuinely useful for diagnosis, but it isn't a dial the auction reads directly.

## Why It Affects What You Pay

Google's auction doesn't simply award the top position to whoever bids most. It uses **Ad Rank**, which combines your bid with your ad quality and several other factors.

The practical consequence: a competitor with better ad quality can win a higher position than you while paying less per click. Or, put the other way, poor quality means you're paying a premium to reach the same people.

## The Three Components

Quality Score is built from three components, each rated Below Average, Average, or Above Average.

### Expected Click-Through Rate

This estimates how likely someone is to click your ad when it shows for this keyword, relative to other advertisers in the same position.

### Ad Relevance

This measures how closely your ad matches the intent behind the keyword.

### Landing Page Experience

This assesses whether the page people land on delivers what the ad promised — relevant content, clear navigation, reasonable load speed, mobile usability, and transparency.

## How to Actually Improve It

**Tighten your ad groups first.** This single change improves ad relevance and expected CTR simultaneously.

**Put the keyword theme into your ad copy.** Not keyword stuffing — just making sure someone searching that term sees their language reflected back.

**Run multiple responsive search ads per ad group.** Two or three RSAs with genuinely different angles gives you comparative data.

**Match landing pages to ad groups, not campaigns.** Sending every ad group to your homepage is the most common landing page problem.

**Check mobile load speed specifically.** Run your actual landing page URL through PageSpeed Insights.

**Add negative keywords consistently.** Weekly search terms review is the habit that prevents irrelevant traffic.

## What Doesn't Improve Quality Score

**Raising your bid.** Bid and quality are separate inputs to Ad Rank.

**Increasing your budget.** Budget controls total spend, not quality or per-click cost.

**Pausing low-scoring keywords to raise your average.** This improves a number on a dashboard without improving anything real.

**Waiting.** Quality Score responds to accumulated performance data on your current setup.

## How Long Improvements Take

Structural changes need time to gather performance data before the score reflects them. Realistically, expect two to four weeks before the numbers move, longer on low-volume keywords where data accumulates slowly.

## A Caveat Worth Holding Onto

Quality Score is a diagnostic, not a goal. The metric that matters is what a customer costs you. Quality Score is useful precisely because improving it usually lowers that cost — but when the two point in different directions, follow the money, not the score.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a good Google Ads Quality Score?**

7 or above is generally healthy. Anything at 5 or below is worth investigating.

**Where do I find Quality Score in Google Ads?**

In the Keywords view, click the Columns icon, and add Quality Score along with Expected CTR, Ad Relevance, and Landing Page Experience.

**Does Quality Score affect Performance Max or Shopping campaigns?**

The reported 1-10 Quality Score applies to Search keywords. Ad quality still influences auctions across campaign types, but you won't see this specific diagnostic column for them.

**Can a new keyword have a low Quality Score with no data?**

New keywords often start with a provisional score based on related signals in your account. It settles as real performance data accumulates.

**Does improving Quality Score guarantee lower costs?**

It usually reduces cost per click, but the auction also depends on competition.
